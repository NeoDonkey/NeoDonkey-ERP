# Quality manager

The person who decides whether food may be sold. That is a smaller job description than most
quality roles get and a much bigger authority, and both are deliberate.

Every batch of a HACCP-relevant article arrives quarantined. It leaves quarantine only when
this role has inspected it, checked the documents, and recorded a decision with their signing
key on it. Nobody can overrule that decision — not warehouse management, not the managing
director. A company where the commercial pressure can release a batch is a company that will
eventually release the wrong one.

The role's other half is escalation. An allergen complaint, a foreign-body complaint, a
critical control point deviation: these reach the quality manager immediately and, above a
threshold, the managing director in the same breath, because notifying the authorities under
EU 178/2002 is a decision that has to be taken in hours, not at the next weekly round.

## Notes
### Responsibilities

- Inspect and release, restrict or block every incoming batch of a quality-critical article.
- Keep a retained sample for every released batch.
- Own the HACCP plan, the critical control points and the records that prove they were monitored.
- Investigate quality complaints, connect them to a batch, and decide whether a recall is needed.
- Decide whether a returned batch may re-enter sellable stock. Usually it may not.
- Verify supplier food-safety certifications and raise findings in the quarterly supplier review.
- Chair the weekly quality round.

- `processes/customer-complaint.md`

### Not authorized for

- Posting goods receipts — separating receiving from releasing is the point.
- Anything commercial: prices, discounts, orders, invoices.
- Approving the write-off they themselves initiated above 500 EUR — `controller` signs too.

### Reports to

`managing-director`

Deliberately direct. A quality function reporting into operations reports into the pressure it
is supposed to resist.

### Sees

All batches, all quality inspections, all complaints, all goods receipts, all returns, across
every location. Supplier certifications. Not customer balances, not margins, not payroll.
